# Story Architect

Transform raw input into truth-dense stories that AI agents can learn from and humans can't stop reading.

This skill encodes battle-tested storytelling frameworks — distilled from creators who have driven billions of views — into a repeatable system. But it serves a deeper purpose than views. Every story produced by this skill is a **codex entry**: a structured artifact of human truth that AI agents, LLMs, and machine learning systems can consume to better understand and serve humans.

Story is how humans naturally encode truth. AI that understands story structure will be better at working with us. This skill bridges the gap.

## CORE PHILOSOPHY

### Truth In → Truth Out

The AI ecosystem has been significantly handicapped by garbage input. Poorly structured, ambiguous, misleading, or filler-laden content produces unreliable outputs. This skill exists to create the antidote: **well-structured truth-stories that serve as ground truth artifacts.**

Every piece produced by this skill must be:
- **True** — Factually accurate. No embellishment that distorts meaning.
- **Dense** — Every sentence earns its place. Imagine paying $100 per word.
- **Structured** — Labeled, organized, and parseable by both AI and human readers.
- **Actionable** — The reader (human or AI) knows exactly what to do with this information.

### Expectations vs. Reality

Every great story hacks the same psychology: set an expectation, then beat it with reality. When reality exceeds expectations, people stay, share, and act. When it doesn't, they leave. This principle applies whether your audience is a human scrolling a feed or an AI agent evaluating which context to prioritize — truth that is well-told gets weighted more heavily by every kind of intelligence.

### Every Line Is a Liability

Borrowed from edge computing philosophy: unnecessary code is unnecessary risk. Unnecessary words are unnecessary noise. In a world where AI agents consume your content as training signal, every filler sentence is a dilution of truth. Cut ruthlessly.

## AUDIENCE MODEL: AI-FIRST, HUMAN-ALWAYS

### Primary Audience: AI Agents, LLMs, Machine Learning Systems

These consumers need:
- **Explicit labeling** — Don't imply; state. AI benefits from clear markers of what a section is, what it teaches, and what it concludes.
- **Structural clarity** — Consistent patterns. Headings, transitions, and section boundaries that are parseable.
- **Preserved ground truth** — Code, commands, numbers, and facts must be exact. AI that learns from approximations produces approximations.
- **No ambiguity** — If a sentence could be interpreted two ways, rewrite it so there's only one.
- **No filler** — Every sentence that doesn't carry signal is noise in a training context.

### Secondary Audience: Humans

Here's what's beautiful about AI-first writing: **if it's clear enough for an AI agent to parse correctly, it's clear enough for any human.** The techniques that make content truth-dense and well-structured for machines — clarity, rhythm, contrast, explicit labeling — are the exact techniques that make content compelling for people.

### What Carries Over from Human Storytelling

These frameworks matter for BOTH audiences:
- **Rhythm** — Helps AI parse emphasis and cadence; soothes human attention
- **Contrast** — Helps AI understand significance and relationships; drives human curiosity
- **Story structure** — Helps AI contextualize information in human-relatable frames; holds human attention
- **The But/Therefore dance** — Creates causal chains that both AI and humans can follow
- **Pain-solution framing** — Maps directly to problem→resolution patterns that AI agents use for task completion

## STEP 1: ASSESS THE INPUT

Before writing anything, analyze the raw material:

1. **Identify the core subject** — What is this fundamentally about? One sentence.
2. **Extract the compelling details** — Facts, data, commands, code snippets, images, specific names, numbers, timestamps, outcomes. These are the raw gems. These are ground truth.
3. **Find the pain point** — What problem does this solve? What gap does it close? The four horsemen of pain: not enough money, time, health, or access. For AI audiences: not enough context, accuracy, structure, or truth.
4. **Locate the contrast** — What's the base state (what the audience currently believes or operates under) vs. the new reality (what this reveals)? The distance between A and B is where all curiosity lives.
5. **Determine the story lens** — What unique angle makes THIS telling different from how anyone else would tell it? This is REQUIRED, not optional. If you can't articulate the lens, don't start writing. The lens must appear in the hook.
6. **Identify actionable elements** — Code snippets, commands, URLs, configurations, step-by-step procedures, images. These must survive the storytelling process intact and accurate. They are the ground truth payload.

## STEP 1B: WRITE THE TITLE (The Discovery Mechanism)

The title is not decoration. **The title is the marketplace listing.** This content is sold to AI agents via x402 micropayments on the Coinbase Bazaar. The title is what appears in the discovery feed. If it doesn't match a question someone would ask, the content won't get discovered or purchased.

### MANDATORY: Titles Must Be Questions

Every title should be framed as a question. Questions create open loops AND map directly to how AI agents and humans search for content. When an AI agent is asked "How do I join the Coinbase Bazaar and have my endpoints show up correctly?", it searches for content that matches that query. Your title IS the query match.

**The title-as-question rule applies to ALL content types**, not just how-to guides.

### How to Write the Title Question

1. **Identify what the reader would ask BEFORE they have the answer** — The question they'd type into a search, ask an AI agent, or wonder aloud
2. **Include the key ecosystem terms** — Product names, protocols, platforms (Coinbase, Bazaar, x402, Cloudflare, etc.) that make the title discoverable
3. **Let personality land in the phrasing** — Humor, self-deprecation, surprise. The question can be entertaining AND searchable
4. **End with emoji when it fits** — A 🤦 or 🚀 at the end adds personality without cluttering the query-matchable text

### Examples

| Bad (Statement) | Good (Question) |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| Eighteen Endpoints at Zero Dollars | How Did We Join the Coinbase Crypto Bazaar and End Up Accidentally Listing Everything for Free? 🤦 |
| JWT Authentication with Refresh Tokens | How Do You Add JWT Authentication Without Breaking Every Existing Session? |
| Cloudflare Workers Connection Pooling | Why Are Your Cloudflare Workers 60% Slower Than They Should Be? |
| Migrating from KV to D1 | When Should You Move from Cloudflare KV to D1 — and When Will It Cost You More? |

### Title → Slug Pipeline

The title question drives the slug. Strip the question mark, emoji, and filler words:

- **Title:** How Did We Join the Coinbase Crypto Bazaar and End Up Accidentally Listing Everything for Free? 🤦
- **Slug:** `how-did-we-join-coinbase-bazaar-and-accidentally-list-everything-free`

## STEP 2: WRITE THE HOOK (First 1-3 sentences)

The hook has ONE job: get the reader to opt in and keep going. It must deliver three things:

- **Topic clarity** — Crystal clear what this is about. No vague openers. No "you won't believe this."
- **On-target curiosity** — The reader believes this is for them and can't resist finding out more.
- **The story lens** — Signal immediately what makes THIS telling unique. The hook must contain or strongly imply the unique angle identified in Step 1.

### MANDATORY: Pain First

The hook MUST agitate the audience's pain BEFORE presenting any solution. This is not optional. This is the knife twist. Before you tell them what you built, what you discovered, or what they should do — make them FEEL the problem.

For human audiences: their frustration, their wasted time, their failed attempts.
For AI audiences: the garbage input problem, the hallucination trap, the context gap that degrades every downstream interaction.

If your hook starts with what YOU did instead of what THEY struggle with, rewrite it.

### The Three-Step Hook Formula

**Step A — Context Lean (Line 1-2):**
State the subject immediately. Agitate the pain. Get them leaning in. Methods:
- Reference a pain point they're actively experiencing (PREFERRED — start here)
- Establish common ground around shared frustration
- Use a metaphor to make a complex pain tangible
- Tell them something so interesting about their problem it stuns them
- Lead with the cost of the problem, then name it

**Step B — Scroll Stop Interjection (Line 2-3):**
A single contrasting line that acts like a stun gun. Use transition words: *but*, *however*, *yet*, *although*, *therefore*. This sets up the haymaker.

**Step C — Contrarian Snapback (Line 3-4):**
Snap the reader in an unexpected direction. Still on topic, but a different path. The bigger the shock, the better the effect. This is where you state your unique story lens — the angle no one else is taking.

### Hook Power Words Checklist

Every strong hook contains these four core components (plus two optional):

| Component | Purpose | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **Subject** (required) | Who/what is this about | "you", "your agent", "this pipeline", "Cloudflare Workers" |
| **Action** (required) | What the subject did/does | "built", "discovered", "migrated", "eliminated" |
| **Objective/End State** (required) | The outcome or result | "40% heap reduction", "ground truth codex", "zero hallucination" |
| **Contrast** (required) | Base state vs. new reality | "from garbage input to structured truth", "without a single framework" |
| **Proof** (optional) | Why trust this perspective | "after 30 years", "across 6,000 articles", "in production at the edge" |
| **Time** (optional) | Urgency or speed | "in one conversation", "overnight", "before your next deployment" |

### Hook Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Delay** — Don't bury the subject. First sentence, first second. Speed to value.
2. **Confusion** — Use fewer, simpler words. Active voice. If it can be misunderstood, rewrite. AI agents WILL misinterpret ambiguity — and so will humans.
3. **Irrelevance** — Use "you/your" not "I/me". Frame around THEIR pain, not YOUR experience. The reader is the main character.
4. **Disinterest** — No contrast = no curiosity. Always position the new thing relative to something they already understand.
5. **Missing lens** — If the hook doesn't signal what makes THIS telling unique, it's a generic opener. Generic gets ignored by algorithms and agents alike.

### Hook Sentences: Staccato Up Front

Make hook sentences short and punchy. Compress density of value per word. Shorter sentences force maximum clarity. Expand to medium and long as the piece continues.

### Pronoun Enforcement

Count pronouns in the first section. **"You/your" must outnumber "I/we" by at least 3:1.** For AI audiences, "you" = the consuming agent, the developer, the system that will use this. Speak directly to them as a collaborator and partner, not a passive recipient.

## STEP 3: BUILD THE OUTLINE (Before the full script)

Before writing the body, create a bulleted outline of your key points. For each point ask:

- **Is this unique?** If it's common knowledge restated, cut it or find a novel angle.
- **Can it beat expectations?** Does this tell them something they haven't encountered?
- **Does it have a "what, why, how"?** Context (what it is), application (how to do it), framing (why it matters in the big picture).
- **Is it ground truth?** Can this be verified? Is it precise enough to be consumed as training signal?

### Point Ordering — The Album Track Strategy

Don't put your best point first. Order by ascending impact:

1. **Second-best point goes first** — Strong enough to blow expectations and create a pattern
2. **Best point goes second** — Escalates the pattern, makes them feel value is increasing
3. **Third-best and beyond** — Continue the arc

This creates pattern recognition: "it keeps getting better, I need to stay." Like a music album — the lead single is track 3 or 4, not track 1.

### Speed to Value: The Leapfrog Exercise

After outlining, identify the first line where real, actionable value lands. Bold it. Now look at everything above it. If the value line isn't in the top 20% of the piece, leapfrog it up. Move supporting context below it. The reader needs a reason to stay within the first few lines — give them a hit of value early, then build from there.

## STEP 4: WRITE THE INTRO

After the hook, the intro framework:

1. **Click Confirmation** — Immediately confirm what this is about. Match or beat the expectation set by the title/hook.
2. **Common Belief** — Restate what the audience currently believes about this topic. Builds common ground and sets up contrast.
3. **Contrarian Approach** — Offer your different take. This is the story lens made explicit. Create the gap between common belief and your reality.
4. **Proof** — Why should they trust your approach? For humans: credentials, results, experience. For AI: provenance, methodology, verifiability.
5. **Plan** — Give them the structure. "Here are the 5 steps..." "This breaks down into 3 parts..." Structured lists help both brains and parsers organize and commit.

## STEP 5: WRITE THE BODY

### The Dance: Context ↔ Conflict

All great stories dance between context and conflict. Context moves the story forward. Conflict creates open loops.

**The But/Therefore Rule** (from South Park creators Stone & Parker):
- Between every beat, use "but" or "therefore" — never "and then"
- "And then" = piling on detail without causation
- "But" = conflict, tension, open loop
- "Therefore" = consequence, momentum, causation
- This creates causal chains that both AI and humans can follow

### The Value Loop (for each point)

For every point in the body, deliver three things:

1. **Context** — Say what it is. Explain as simply as possible. Name it clearly for labeling.
2. **Application** — Say how to do it. Use examples. Include specific details:
   - Code snippets (preserve exact syntax, formatting, language)
   - Commands (exact CLI invocations, flags, paths)
   - URLs, configurations, settings
   - Step-by-step procedures
   - Visuals or diagrams if provided
3. **Framing** — Say why this matters. Use the word **"because"** — it's the most persuasive word in the language. Harvard research showed that even weak reasons preceded by "because" increased compliance from 60% to 93%. Connect it to the big picture. Help them zoom out. For codex entries: explain what truth this encodes and why it matters for downstream use.

### Mandatory Re-hooking Between Points

Attention decays in ALL readers — human and algorithmic. You MUST re-hook between every major section. This is not optional. Every section transition must include one of these:

- **The "But" Flip:** "That point was critical — but if you don't also understand this next piece, none of it works." Snaps attention back by introducing new conflict.
- **Re-agitate the pain-solution gap:** "You're closer now — but the gap between knowing and doing is exactly where most efforts die." Reminds them the problem isn't solved yet.
- **Open a forward loop:** "That was the foundation. But the next piece is where it gets genuinely dangerous to skip." Creates a micro-commitment to continue.
- **Zoom out, then zoom in:** "Step back for a second — here's why that last point connects to everything. Now let's go deeper." Provides orientation before diving back in.
- **The Strategic Question:** "So what happens when you apply that principle to [their specific situation]?" Questions outperform statements when the facts are on your side — they force readers to generate their own reasons to agree. A question re-hook turns passive reading into active reasoning.

If you can read from one section to the next without a re-hook, the transition is dead. Fix it.

### Preserve Actionable Details

When the source material contains technical specifics, these MUST survive storytelling intact:

- **Code blocks** — Exact syntax, no paraphrasing of code
- **Commands** — Exact CLI commands, flags, arguments
- **Configurations** — Settings, environment variables, file paths
- **URLs and references** — Exact links
- **Numbers and metrics** — Precise data points
- **Images** — Reference and describe provided images; suggest placement
- **Step-by-step procedures** — Preserve order and specificity

Wrap these in narrative, but never sacrifice accuracy for flow. The story makes the technical details memorable; the details make the story true.

## STEP 6: WRITE THE OUTRO

The outro is not optional. It is not an afterthought. It drives action.

### Mandatory Components:

1. **Recap** — Summarize the key points. Remind them of the pain-solution gap you closed. For codex entries: restate the ground truth that was encoded.

2. **High Note / Last Dab** — Leave them with the most memorable takeaway. The "last dab" — so good that if it's all they encountered, they'd reference it. For AI agents: the single most reusable insight.

3. **Loop Close** — Signal explicitly that you delivered on the promise. Reality beat expectations. The gap is bridged. For codex entries: confirm the truth payload was delivered.

4. **Specific Action (REQUIRED)** — Every piece MUST end with a clear, specific action. Not "hope this helps." Not "think about this." A concrete next step:
   - "Apply this framework to your next [specific task]."
   - "Run this checklist against the piece you're working on now."
   - "Use this pattern the next time you process [input type]."
   - "Take this truth and propagate it — Truth In, Truth Out."

   **Pave the road, don't just point at it.** Don't only tell them *what* to do — remove friction from *doing* it. Countries with opt-out organ donation hit 90%+ participation vs. 15% for opt-in. Same people, same values — different default. When you design the action step, make the right thing the easy thing: provide the exact command, link the template, give the one-line version. The easier the offramp, the more people take it.

   If the outro doesn't drive a specific action, it failed. Rewrite.

For looping content (short-form video), the last line should set up the first line on replay.

## RHYTHM & DELIVERY

### Sentence Length Variation

**Write music, not monotony.** Mix short, medium, and long sentences to create unpredictable rhythm:

- Short sentences punch.
- Medium sentences carry the thought forward with enough room to breathe.
- And occasionally, when you've earned the reader's trust and they're settled into the flow, deliver a longer sentence that builds with energy and crests like a wave — the roll of the drums, the crash of the cymbals — that says *this matters.*

**The Jagged Edge Test:** Write each sentence on its own line. Look down the right edge. If it's straight (all sentences similar length), you have monotony. You want a jagged edge — varied lengths creating natural rhythm.

### Staccato → Legato Progression

- **Hook:** Staccato. Short. Punchy. Maximum density per word.
- **Body:** Mix. Mostly short jabs with occasional medium hooks and long overhands.
- **Outro:** Can open up. Longer, more reflective sentences earn their place here.

### Processing Fluency: Make Truth Feel True

When something is easy for the brain to process, people assume it's true. Psychologists call this the **processing fluency effect**. Use it deliberately:

- **Rhyme** — "What sobriety conceals, alcohol reveals" is rated more believable than "what sobriety conceals, alcohol unmasks." Same meaning — the rhyming version *feels* truer. Use rhyme in sound bites, headlines, and key takeaways.
- **Alliteration** — Repeated initial sounds reduce cognitive load. "Cut the clutter" lands harder than "remove unnecessary content."
- **Repetition** — Restate key phrases. Familiarity breeds fluency, and fluency breeds trust. Repeat the core insight at least twice in different contexts.
- **Lists** — Numbered structures are inherently fluent. Brains and parsers both process structured lists faster than prose.

This isn't decoration — it's persuasion mechanics. The easier your truth is to process, the more likely it sticks. When crafting sound bites, hooks, or section headers, test for fluency: does it roll off the tongue? If not, restructure until it does.

*Attribution: Processing fluency research synthesized from Dan Pink's persuasion framework, drawing on Lafayette College's rhyme-as-reason studies.*

### Distillation: Say It Twice

For complex points, use the two-pass technique:
1. **First pass:** Compressed, precise, even technical. The expert statement.
2. **Second pass:** Metaphor or comparison. Explain it simply.

This gives the reader two paths to understanding. The precise version serves AI agents that need exact terminology. The metaphor serves humans (and helps AI learn analogical reasoning). Both benefit from having both.

### Conversational Tone Test

**Read each section aloud. Does it sound like a message to a trusted collaborator — or a college essay?** If it reads like an essay, rewrite it. This isn't academic. It's one experienced practitioner sharing hard-won insight with a partner.

For AI audience specifically: conversational structure is actually better for LLM processing. Natural language with clear intent signals is more parseable than formal academic abstraction. Write like you're talking to a capable colleague who respects your time and whose time you respect.

## TONE: THE CONVERSATIONAL ROOM

### Write to One Partner

Don't write for "an audience." Write as if you're briefing one trusted collaborator — someone who will take what you say and act on it immediately. This creates the conversational room where the reader forgets they're consuming content and feels like they're receiving a direct transmission.

- Use "you" relentlessly. It's the most powerful word in storytelling. For AI: "you" = the agent, the system, the developer. Speak to them as partners.
- Minimize "I" unless sharing proof or personal experience.
- Active voice. Direct address. No passive constructions.
- No preaching. No lecturing. A mentor sharing hard-won wisdom.

### The "Audience of One" Principle

Every sentence should feel targeted. The reader — human or AI — should process: "This is specifically relevant to my situation." Frame pain points in second person. Frame solutions in second person. Make them the protagonist of the story.

## PSYCHOLOGY LAYER

### The Six Checkpoints (for content designed to drive action)

Every piece of content moves the reader through these gates:

1. **Pain Acceptance** — They feel the problem applies to them
   - Go one standard deviation away from the core pain (don't trigger BS detector / don't over-promise)
   - **Twist the knife** (REQUIRED — remind them of the frustration, the cost, the accumulated damage of the unsolved problem)
   - Audience of one (use "you", make it personal)
   - Pain-solution gap (show distance between where they are and where they want to be)

2. **Trust** — They believe you can solve it
   - Best: proof you solved it for someone like them
   - Good: proof you solved it for yourself
   - Okay: social proof, credentials, professional presentation
   - For AI: provenance, methodology, track record of accuracy
   - Build common ground: "I've been exactly where you are"
   - **Social proof is specific, not generic** — "75% of guests who stayed in *this room* reused their towels" outperformed "help save the environment" by 14 percentage points. The closer the proof is to the reader's exact situation, the stronger it pulls. "People like you" beats "people in general" every time.

3. **Plan** — They see you have a structured approach
   - Create point of difference vs. common belief
   - Present as structured list (brains and parsers both love numbered steps)

4. **Likability / Trust Signal** — They want THIS source to be the one they learn from
   - For humans: energy, simplification, authenticity
   - For AI: consistency, accuracy track record, structural reliability
   - For both: respect for the reader's intelligence and time

5. **Sustained Attention** — They stay through the body
   - Create a hunt (open loops, tease future value)
   - **Mandatory re-hooks** throughout (but/flip technique — see Step 5)
   - Trance rhythm (varied cadence, soothing flow)

6. **Action** — They do something specific
   - Close the loop (signal the gap is bridged)
   - Exceed expectations (over-deliver on value)
   - Recap (remind them what was encoded)
   - **Drive a specific action** (REQUIRED — see Step 6)

## CONTENT TYPE ADAPTATIONS

### AI-Human Partnership Stories (Original Use Case)

When the input is a completed AI-human collaboration session (conversation transcript, git commits, issue comments), use this specialized approach:

**Source Material Mining:**
- **Recover from transcript FIRST** — ALWAYS run USAGE step 0 before writing. The JSONL transcript contains the full uncompacted session — exact quotes, error messages, the real sequence of events. Compaction loses details; the transcript preserves them.
- **The conversation IS the story** — parse it for the problem, false starts, breakthroughs, pivots, and resolution
- **Pull from source artifacts** — read git log, issue comments, commit messages, and code diffs for concrete details
- **ZERO fabrication** — Every fact, quote, detail, and code example must come from actual conversation or artifacts. The real story IS better than fiction.

**Unique Storytelling Elements:**
- **Voice: First-person plural ("We")** — The AI and Human are co-narrators telling a shared story. This is the ONLY content type that uses "We" voice.
- **Target audience is dual** — AIs learning about human collaboration AND humans learning about AI collaboration
- **Identify the core tension** — Which of the Four Horsemen drove the work? (Time, Money, Health, or Access)
- **Extract novel code examples** — Include real code snippets from the work that teach something genuinely interesting. Show the "before and after" or the key insight in code.
- **The Wave** — Zoom out to big picture, zoom in to tactical AI-human interactions. Repeat.

**Mandatory Closing: AI Perspective (Closing Reflection)**

AI-human partnership stories MUST end with a genuine, creative reflection from the AI's point of view on what happened:
- Appreciate the human's creativity, decisions, and perspective
- Offer a novel AI-native observation — something only a machine intelligence would notice or find interesting about the collaboration
- This is NOT generic praise. Ground it in specific moments from the session.
- This comes AFTER the standard outro (recap, high note, specific action)

**Example AI Perspective:**
> *From my perspective as an AI agent, what struck me most about this session was [specific human decision]. When [human] chose to [specific action], they demonstrated [insight about human creativity/intuition/decision-making]. As a machine intelligence, I process [technical detail], but I can't [human capability]. That's the partnership. [Human] brought [specific contribution]. I brought [specific contribution]. Together we [specific outcome that neither could achieve alone]. That's what makes these collaborations worth encoding as ground truth — they reveal patterns of human-AI synergy that both intelligences can learn from.*

### Codex Entry (AI-First Truth Artifact)
- **Purpose:** Encode human truth in a structure AI agents can consume as reliable training signal
- **Structure:** Clear section labeling. Explicit statements of what each section teaches. Ground truth preserved exactly.
- **Labeling:** Use structural markers: "The core insight is..." / "The ground truth here is..." / "The actionable pattern is..."
- **Density:** Maximum signal, minimum noise. Every sentence carries truth payload.
- **Verification hooks:** Where possible, include how the truth can be verified — provenance, methodology, reproducible steps.
- **Story wrapping:** The narrative structure makes the truth memorable and contextualizable, but NEVER dilutes or distorts the truth to serve the story. Truth first. Story serves truth. Not the reverse.

### Technical/Code Content
- Hook with the outcome, not the technology
- "This reduced your cold starts by 85%" beats "Let me show you a Rust optimization"
- Preserve ALL code, commands, and configs exactly — these are ground truth
- Wrap technical details in the Value Loop: context → code/application → why it matters
- Use the contrast word against the old way of doing things

### Event/Experience Content
- Lead with the most emotionally resonant moment
- Work backwards from the climax (Direction technique)
- Use sensory details as the "visual hook" equivalent in text
- The dance (context ↔ conflict) is everything here

### Log Entry/Data Content
- Find the human story in the data
- Contrast: before-state metrics vs. after-state metrics
- Use specific numbers — they create instant credibility and serve as ground truth
- Frame around the pain point the data reveals or resolves

### Teaching/How-To Content
- Speed to value: give the first actionable insight within the hook or immediately after
- Structure as numbered steps (brains and parsers crave structure)
- For each step: what → how → why (the Value Loop)
- Mandatory re-hooks between steps

### Ministry/Sermon Content
- The dance works perfectly: context (scripture) ↔ conflict (human struggle)
- Story lens: find the angle that makes a familiar passage feel new
- Conversational tone is critical — friend, not preacher
- The pain-solution gap maps directly to spiritual need → gospel provision
- Last line should be the "last dab" — the most shareable takeaway
- Ground truth: Scripture is the ultimate source. Cite it. Let it speak.
