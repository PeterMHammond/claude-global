# StoryBrand

The user invoked this skill to create or improve a story, pitch, or message using Donald Miller's StoryBrand framework. Ask what they need, then apply the right framework with concrete output.

## First: Ask What They Need

Use AskUserQuestion to identify the mode:
- **PEACE** — 5 sound bites for a business/product
- **Guide Backstory** — origin story (About Us, founder video, sales presentation)
- **Presentation/Pitch** — structure any talk or sales pitch
- **Content Audit** — review existing copy for framework problems
- **Sound Bite** — compress a single idea into a repeatable phrase

---

## The Non-Negotiable Foundation

**Customer = Hero. Brand = Guide.** Every framework below serves this one principle.

The fatal error: making the brand the hero ("We are the best at..."). When the brand is the hero, the customer has no role — they leave. When the customer is the hero, your job is to play Yoda: empathy + authority + plan → their transformation.

Story arc: **Hero in the Hole → Guide Appears → Guide Gives Plan → Hero Acts → Transformation**

## PEACE Framework (5 Sound Bites)

Each element is itself a sound bite — short, vivid, specific, repeatable. Deploy as a sequence — each builds the trust the next requires. Never dump all five into one piece.

**P — Problem** (external situation)
- Specific enough to trigger "That's me." Generic = no response. Specific = instant recognition.
- Template: "Most [customer type] are [specific problem] and [consequence they don't realize]."
- YNAB: *"Most people are spending more than they make and they don't even know it."*

**E — Empathy** (internal/emotional consequence)
- Not "we understand your situation" — "we know how it *feels*."
- This is the trust accelerator: moves from "they know my problem" → "they know how I feel about it."
- YNAB: *"It feels embarrassing to not know where your money went. Like you should know better by now."*

**A — Answer** (transformation vehicle, not features)
- The response to P — not a feature list, the category of transformation.
- Always attach **"because"** — the most persuasive word in the language. "Because" drops resistance even when the reason is weak (Harvard copy machine study: compliance jumped from 60% to 93%). Your Answer should follow the pattern: "[What we do] because [why it works]."
- YNAB: *"We give you a system that tells you exactly where every dollar is going before you spend it — because you can't fix what you can't see."*

**C — Change** (life after — specific, time-bound, felt)
- Vague change doesn't convert. Specific, measurable, internal-state change does.
- YNAB: *"People who use YNAB stop living paycheck to paycheck within the first month."*

**E — End Result** (identity transformation — who do they become?)
- The highest-level brand promise. Closes the full arc from problem to transformed person.
- YNAB: *"People who budget don't just manage money better — they stop feeling anxious about the future."*

**Three-Phase Campaign Deployment:**
- Phase 1 (Curiosity): P + E — open loops, don't close them
- Phase 2 (Enlightenment): A — educate in depth
- Phase 3 (Commitment): C + E — show transformation, specific CTA. **Make the action effortless** — don't just tell them what to do, remove friction from doing it. Countries with opt-out organ donation hit 90%+ vs 15% for opt-in. Same people, different default. Design your CTA so the right action is the easiest action.

## Sound Bite Craft

A sound bite is a cognitive shortcut: under 10 words, vivid, emotionally resonant, repeatable. Working memory holds ~4 items. A paragraph puts 15-20 in — most gets dropped. A sound bite puts 1. It wins by default.

**Test:** Can a stranger repeat it verbatim 24 hours after hearing it?

**Compression Process:**
1. Write the full idea (paragraph)
2. Identify the single most important concept
3. Find the most *vivid* word (not most accurate — most vivid)
4. Compress to under 10 words
5. Remove all qualifiers: "quite," "very," "a bit," "kind of"
6. Test on a stranger — can they repeat it tomorrow?

**Processing Fluency — Make It Feel True:**
When something is easy for the brain to process, people rate it as more accurate and trustworthy. Leverage this:
- **Rhyme** — "What sobriety conceals, alcohol reveals" is rated more believable than the non-rhyming equivalent. Rhyme when it fits: "No pain, no gain." "Fake it till you make it."
- **Alliteration** — Repeated initial sounds reduce cognitive load. "Cut the clutter" beats "remove unnecessary content."
- **Repetition** — Familiarity breeds fluency. Repeat the core phrase across touchpoints.

A great sound bite exploits fluency naturally. If it's hard to say, it's hard to remember.

**Examples of the standard:** "1,000 songs in your pocket." "Just do it." "Murder your thirst." "Eat mor chikin."

## Guide Backstory: 8-Part Formula

Used for: About Us pages, founder videos, speaking engagements, sales presentations. Creates neural coupling — the listener's brain synchronizes with the storyteller's, generating felt trust rather than evaluated trust.

The rule: your story only matters insofar as it answers "can they help me?" Center every part on the customer's problem, not your achievement.

1. **The Hole** — You were once in the exact situation the customer is in now. Specific and vivid. *"I was $40,000 in debt with no idea how to get out."*
2. **The Calling** — Something pulled you toward solving this problem: a mentor, a crisis, a moment of clarity.
3. **The Refinement** — You didn't just escape once. You tested, refined, discarded what didn't work. Personal experience → professional methodology.
4. **The Dark Night** — A moment when even your solution seemed inadequate. You nearly quit. This is the vulnerability that makes you human, not a brochure. Without it: unbelievable. *"There was a year I thought I would never get ahead."*
5. **The Miracle** — A breakthrough that changed trajectory. *"Then I discovered one principle that unlocked everything else."*
6. **The Breakthrough** — Life changed in a specific, measurable way. *"I went from $40K in debt to six months of savings for the first time."*
7. **The Better World** — You began helping others. Establishes scale of authority. *"We've now helped 3,000 families eliminate debt."*
8. **The Moral** — State the principle explicitly. This is your positioning. *"Getting out of debt isn't a willpower problem — it's a system problem. Most people have never been given the system."*

**The Moral as Positioning Template:**
> "Most people think [problem] is a [wrong frame: willpower/talent/budget] problem. It is actually a [correct frame: system/mindset/tool] problem. That's what we solve."

**Deployment:** Full 8-part for About Us, founder video, high-ticket sales. Compressed (Hole + Breakthrough + Moral) for homepage.

## Presentation / Pitch Structure

**3 structural moves — in order:**

1. **Open with the problem — as a question when possible** — before name, title, company, credentials. Questions outperform statements because they force the audience to generate their own reasons to agree. *"Are you spending more than you make and don't even know it?"* beats *"Most people spend more than they make."* Reagan won an election with one question: *"Are you better off now than you were four years ago?"* You have 6-8 seconds before the brain tunes out. A question hooks faster than a declaration.

2. **Open a story loop** — naming the problem creates an unanswered question the audience cannot stop wanting resolved. You are now obligated to close it (the "obligatory scene"). Don't open loops you won't close.

3. **Make the problem worse before solving it** — escalate two layers:
   - **Physical**: the tangible, concrete problem
   - **Emotional**: the internal consequence — embarrassment, self-doubt, social cost
   Ramp the conflict. Keep it elevated. ~80% of fiction is conflict. That's not accident — that's craft.

**Hook and Set:** Problem attracts attention (hook). Escalating the problem locks attention (sets the hook). Solve too early = hook was never set — audience drifts.

**Close with a vivid picture of life after** — specific, sensory, concrete. Not "things will be better" but "when someone knocks, the dog doesn't even get up." This closing image is what they carry out.

## Content Audit Diagnostics

Run these on any existing copy, page, or script:

**Airplane Reader Test:** Could a stranger understand what you do and who you help within 30 seconds? If not — the message failed, regardless of accuracy.

**Hero/Guide Inversion Check:** Count sentences about the company's excellence vs. the customer's transformation. The ratio should favor the customer. Most businesses fail this.

**Past-Tense Rule:** "I used to have this problem" = credible guide. "I also have this problem" = strips your authority to lead anyone out of it.

**Loop Closure Check:** Every problem named must be resolved. Every story opened must be closed. Unclosed loops feel like broken promises.

**Sound Bite Test:** After reading, what one phrase can the reader repeat? If there isn't one — there's no sound bite. Rewrite around one repeatable core.

---

## Output Standard

Always produce concrete, usable output — not advice about what to write. If they ask for PEACE sound bites: write all 5. If they ask for a guide backstory: write all 8 parts. If they ask to audit copy: identify specific failures and rewrite the problem sentences. Draft first, refine with them second.
